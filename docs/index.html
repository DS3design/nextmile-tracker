Next Mile Tracker

Your tracker shows how many miles you've run this year, straight from your watch. It works with Garmin, Apple Watch, Fitbit / Pixel Watch and Polar. More brands arrive in free updates that install themselves.

Setup (about 5 minutes)

You need your phone and your home WiFi password. The tracker's screen tells you what to do at each step.

  1. Plug it in. Use the USB cable and any phone charger. The screen lights up and says Welcome! with a square code.
  2. Connect it to your WiFi. The screen lists the WiFi networks nearby. Tap your home WiFi, type its password on the screen's keyboard and tap Connect.
    • Not in the list? Tap Look again. If your WiFi has two names, pick the one without "5G".
    • Rather use your phone? Tap Use my phone, point your camera at the code and tap Join.
  3. Connect your watch app. The tracker says WiFi connected! and shows a new code. Make sure your phone is back on your home WiFi, point your camera at the code, and pick the app your watch uses. Then follow the steps for your watch below.
  4. Done. The tracker checks for new miles every 30 minutes and starts a fresh count every January 1.

Linking your watch

Garmin

Tap Open Garmin Connect, sign in, tap Create Key, choose 1 year and copy the key (Garmin only shows it once). Paste it on the tracker's page and tap Link. Garmin emails you before the key expires; make a new one and paste it on the settings page the same way.

Fitbit / Pixel Watch

Tap Continue with Google, sign in with the account your watch uses and allow activity access. Runs appear after your watch syncs with the Fitbit / Google Health app.

Polar

Tap Connect with Polar, sign in to Polar Flow and accept. Polar only shares runs from after you link, so add the miles you'd already run this year on the settings page.

Apple Watch

Apple has no web connection, so a free iPhone Shortcut sends your miles to the tracker once a day. The tracker's page shows a tracker address; copy it. In the Shortcuts app: Automation → + → Time of Day (daily, Run Immediately). Add Find Health Samples (Workouts, Running, this year), Calculate Statistics (Sum of Distance in miles) and Get Contents of URL (POST, JSON body with year, miles and runs, URL = tracker address). Your iPhone must be home on the same WiFi when it runs.

Using it

  • Tap the screen to show a QR code for the settings page, where you can set your own yearly goal, change the name shown, the brightness, the time zone, or choose whether it counts runs, walks and hikes, or both. Tap again to go back.
  • Colors: tap the screen, then tap Colors to cycle through 8 color themes (Next Mile, Daylight, Ocean, Neon, Sunset, Ultraviolet, Flamingo, Gold Medal). You can also pick one on the settings page.
  • Goal bar: by default your goal is to beat last year's miles by about 10%. Set your own number on the settings page any time.
  • Hold the screen for 10 seconds to erase WiFi, your watch link and settings and start setup again (for example, before giving it to someone else).
  • A small red dot in the corner means the last update didn't reach your watch account. It retries on its own.

Troubleshooting

My WiFi isn't in the list

The tracker only works on 2.4 GHz WiFi. Most routers have both; if yours shows two names, choose the one without "5G". Mesh systems usually work as-is.

After signing in, the page won't load

Your phone must be on the same home WiFi as the tracker. Switch WiFi on, then tap Finish linking on the page, or scan the code on the tracker again.

A run is missing

Check it shows in your watch's app as a run (trail and treadmill runs count too). The tracker updates every 30 minutes; you can also tap the screen, scan the code and press Refresh miles now.

I moved or got a new router

If the screen says WiFi not found or shows a red NO WIFI, tap it once and the WiFi list comes back so you can pick your new WiFi. Your watch link and goal are kept.
